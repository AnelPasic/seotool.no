## Non-negotiable standards

1. Norsk klarhet: a real buyer understands what is offered and the practical outcome immediately.
2. Kommersiell logikk / **Commercial Value Gate on sales-intent copy**: lead with a desirable buyer outcome and why hiring the provider is worth more than doing visible tasks alone. Make delivery believable, timing honest, ownership/effort clear and the approved next step easy; avoid the 'I can update this myself' response. Value Equation diagnoses outcome x likelihood vs delay x effort, not copy to quote or guaranteed results. Educational/how-to content must still genuinely help.
3. Honesty and evidence: no invented facts, rankings, reviews, guarantees, claims, AI mentions, prices or proof.
4. SEO/AIO and CRO: content must answer its search/buyer question and support an appropriate, truthful next action.
5. No forced jokes; reject humour that obscures the answer or disrespects the buyer.

## Supported presets

- **Nøktern (noktern):** plain, precise, unambiguous. For facts, prices, proof, policies, offers and form/error controls.
- **Balansert (balansert):** direct Norwegian explanation with a real buyer situation and commercial relevance. Suggested default for NEW customer sites; OWNER chooses final.
- **Spiss (spiss, level 3):** one sharp dry observation if it strengthens comprehension. Do not make every paragraph snarky.
- **Spiss+ (spissplus, level 4):** a clearer contrast and a memorable, concrete analogy illustrating a genuine commercial failure mode. More insight, not more jokes or noise.
- **Spiss+ komisk (spisspluskomisk, level 5):** deliberately funny and still accurate. Use an unexpected but recognizable real-world image, dry comic twist or short punchline after the useful point. The reader should genuinely have a reason to smile, not merely agree with a sharp observation. Do not mock buyers, imply false outcomes or add a joke to every paragraph.
- **Spiss+ komisk+ (spisspluskomiskplus, level 6):** the boldest **editorial** option, not a new technical feature. After a crystal-clear point, land one surprising, absurd-but-recognizable image with a genuinely funny punchline and a concrete business consequence. Favor original situational humor and occasional self-irony over standard agency jokes. If it only sounds clever, it is still level 4 or 5. Keep it rare, brief and respectful; never compromise buyer clarity, facts, legal/price/proof language or the CTA.
- **Spiss+ Hysterisk (spissplushysterisk, level 7):** a genuine **high-comedy brief**, not a sharper marketing sentence. The selected output needs an original comedic **premise -> escalation -> hard left-turn/punchline** (or a comparably strong original reversal, character exchange or callback). Build a ridiculous but recognizable situation that has internal logic. A mild quip, one dry simile, a predictable 'X is like Y' comparison or an unrelated random absurdity is **level 4-6, NOT level 7**. For the writing pass, privately explore a few *different* comic mechanisms, discard weak premises, and read the final beat as something a person might actually retell; don't certify a laugh without real audience feedback. Humor may **lead** the copy, rather than always following a factual paragraph. On an SEO Hero, H1 must still identify the service and why the buyer cares; the larger comic setup may land in support text. Suitable for SoMe, campaigns **and** a site/section/Hero that the OWNER explicitly assigns level 7. Never silently downgrade a saved level-7 selection; if editorial/SEO facts make it unworkable, explain the conflict and supply an alternative, not a tame draft passed off as hysterical. Target agency habits and industry absurdities, never real buyers; keep exaggerated scenes clearly fictional. No copied standup/Chuck Norris jokes, made-up proof, guarantees or comedy in contracts, pricing, results, forms, health, safety or legal copy.

For this brand, the OWNER can choose different values. Do not copy another client's brand personality or Spiss+ setting. The preset level is an editorial label, not a numeric claim or a 0-3 per-dimension intensity. There is no universal “one joke per FAQ”.

## How to apply

- Hero: identify real service, buyer value and action. For a commercial Hero, H1 sells the outcome and ingress explains why a professional takes responsibility rather than handing the buyer a DIY task list. Keep process and effort credible; tone does not replace the Commercial Value Gate. Normally restrain edge, but **honor explicit level-7 Hero part choices**: a real comedy setup may lead while H1 remains standalone recognizable. Do not neuter Hysterisk into a polite quip.
- Services/sales: real buyer outcome, relevant scope, correct promises.
- Educational/explainer: when fitting, use vivid real examples and a dry observation to make the lesson stick.
- FAQ: answer first, then optional sharp observation; keep actual policy/price/scope accurate.
